Topics in Microeconomics M2

My teaching theme: Transport, Spatial Inequality and Demographic Change
Teaching: Co-instructor with Alex Newnham

During Semester 1 of 2026-2027, I am co-teaching Topics in Microeconomics at ENS de Lyon with Alex Newnham. I am responsible for approximately half of the course. My part focuses on how transport infrastructure shapes economic geography, from the development of transport networks to their unequal effects and eventual withdrawal.

We will look at why some cities and regions become better connected than others, who benefits from transport investment, and what happens to communities when infrastructure or public services are reduced. We will draw on examples from France, China, and other countries, and use empirical research to discuss economic mechanisms and the challenges of identifying causal effects.

This page is an editable outline of my teaching sessions, not an official or final ENS de Lyon syllabus. Session 1 is confirmed; Sessions 2-4 and the readings remain provisional.

Outline of my teaching sessions

Confirmed

Session 1 Building Networks and Making Places

Can transport infrastructure create growth and new centres?

How administrative decisions and transport networks shape the geography of economic activity. Historical cases from France and China, trade costs, market access, and evidence on railway expansion and development.

Planned

Session 2 Unequal Effects of Transport Infrastructure

How transport investments affect connected and bypassed places differently. High-speed rail and other projects provide cases for thinking about the distribution of economic benefits, accessibility, and local development.

Planned

Session 3 Infrastructure Withdrawal and Local Effects

Why transport or other local public services are reduced or closed, what these changes mean for accessibility and local economies, and how effects may differ with the available alternatives.

Planned

Session 4 Transport Residential Sorting and Spatial Inequality

How transport connections interact with residential location, differences in income, access to opportunities, and segregation across neighbourhoods.

Selected illustrative readings

These readings illustrate the themes of the sessions. They are not yet a final required reading list.

  • Chambru, C., E. Henry, and B. Marx (2024). “The Dynamic Consequences of State Building: Evidence from the French Revolution.” American Economic Review 114(11), 3578-3622. Session 1.
  • Bai, Y. and R. Jia (2023). “The Economic Consequences of Political Hierarchy: Evidence from Regime Changes in China, AD 1000-2000.” Review of Economics and Statistics 105(3), 626-645. Session 1.
  • Donaldson, D. (2018). “Railroads of the Raj: Estimating the Impact of Transportation Infrastructure.” American Economic Review 108(4-5), 899-934. Session 1.
  • Qin, Y. (2017). “No County Left Behind? The Distributional Impact of High-Speed Rail Upgrades in China.” Journal of Economic Geography 17(3), 489-520. Possible Session 2.
  • Di Cataldo, M. and G. Romani (2024). “Rational Cuts? The Local Impact of Closing Undersized Schools.” Regional Science and Urban Economics 109, 104057. Possible Session 3.
